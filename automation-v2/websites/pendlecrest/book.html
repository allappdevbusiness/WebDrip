Posting it in

Pack it like it’s going across the country, because it might be.

Watches travel well when they can’t move. A small, sturdy box with plenty of padding is safer than a big box with a little. Push the crown in and wrap the watch in a soft cloth first, so the bubble wrap can’t scratch the crystal.

Use a tracked, insured service and insure it for its full replacement value. Keep the receipt until we email to say it has arrived, which we do the same working day.

Before you book

Six things people ask at the counter.

Do I need to book, or can I just walk in?
Walk-ins are welcome whenever the counter is open. A booked slot simply means you won’t queue behind someone else’s bracelet sizing on a busy Saturday or Thursday evening.
What should I bring to the counter?
The watch, any spare links or the original strap, and old service papers if you have them. A note of when it was last serviced helps us judge the state of the oils before we open it.
How do I pay, and when?
Nothing is paid at drop-off. Once you approve the written estimate we start work, and you pay by card or bank transfer when the watch is finished and you’re happy with it.
Is my watch insured while you have it?
Yes, up to £15,000 per watch while it’s in the workshop and on its insured return journey. Tell us at drop-off if it’s worth more and we’ll arrange extra cover.
Can someone else collect it for me?
Of course. They just need the paper ticket or the ticket number, and a name that matches the one you gave us. We’ll check the name before handing it over.
What if I change my mind after approving the estimate?
Ring or email the bench straight away. If we haven’t started, there’s nothing to pay. If we have, we only charge for the work already done, and we’ll tell you the amount before closing the watch up.
