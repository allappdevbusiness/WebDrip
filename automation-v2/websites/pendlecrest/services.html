Inside a full service

Eight stages, about eleven hours of bench time.

  1. Stage 1 of 8

    Inspection

    Timing machine readings before anything is touched: rate, amplitude and beat error, so we can compare after.

  2. Stage 2 of 8

    Case opened

    The movement comes out of the case. The dial and hands are removed and stored in your covered tray.

  3. Stage 3 of 8

    Full strip-down

    Every wheel, bridge, spring and screw comes apart, often 100 to 200 parts, each checked under magnification.

  4. Stage 4 of 8

    Five-bath clean

    Parts go through a cleaning machine: two cleaning baths, then three rinses, then warm-air drying.

  5. Stage 5 of 8

    Reassembly and oiling

    Up to seven oils and greases, each applied in tiny amounts. Jewels get a drop smaller than a pinhead.

  6. Stage 6 of 8

    Regulation

    Rate adjusted in five positions until it sits within a few seconds a day, with amplitude healthy.

  7. Stage 7 of 8

    Case and seals

    New gaskets, the crown and case back resealed, then a dry-air pressure test to its rated depth.

  8. Stage 8 of 8

    Seven-day wear test

    A week on the winder and in the drawer, checked every morning, before we ring you to collect.

Words on your estimate

A short glossary of watch parts.

Estimates name parts precisely so you know what you’re paying for. These are the eight words that come up most often.

The coiled spring in the barrel that stores the energy. Over decades it loses strength, which shortens the power reserve.

The oscillator that beats several times a second and decides how fast the watch runs. Its spring is finer than a hair and easily magnetised.

The pallet fork and escape wheel that release the gear train one tick at a time. Most of the oiling skill goes here.

Synthetic rubies used as low-friction bearings for the pivots. A 17-jewel watch has jewels at every important point.

How far the balance swings, measured in degrees. Low amplitude usually means dirty oil, a tired spring or wear.

How evenly the tick and tock are spaced. We adjust it close to zero for steady timekeeping.

Rubber seals at the case back, crown and crystal that keep water and dust out. They harden with age, so they’re replaced at every service.

The levers and wheels behind the crown that switch between winding and setting the time.

Care guide

Ten habits for a longer-lived watch.

Five things to do and five to avoid. None of them cost anything, and all of them push the next service further away.

Do

  • Wind a hand-wound watch fully every morning, gently, until you feel resistance.
  • Give a stopped automatic 20–30 turns of the crown before putting it on.
  • Keep it in a dry box or pouch, out of direct sunlight, when you aren’t wearing it.
  • Rinse a dive watch in fresh water after swimming in the sea.
  • Have the water resistance tested once a year if it gets wet.

Avoid

  • Don’t set the date between 9pm and 3am on a mechanical calendar.
  • Don’t operate the crown or pushers under water.
  • Don’t leave it on a phone, laptop lid or magnetic charger.
  • Don’t wear it in the sauna or hot tub: heat swells the seals.
  • Don’t let a dead battery sit inside a quartz watch for months.

Guarantee and policies

What we promise, in plain words.

Every service and repair carries a 12-month guarantee, starting from the day you collect.

  • 12-month guarantee

    Everything we serviced or replaced is covered for 12 months from collection. If it stops, loses time beyond the quoted tolerance or lets water in, we put it right free of charge.

  • Written estimate first

    Every estimate is itemised and in writing. If we find something new once the watch is open, we stop and ask before doing more.

  • Free estimate, fair if declined

    Estimates are free. If you decline, we close the watch and return it as it came, at no charge. Postal returns cost £12 for insured delivery.

  • Insured at all times

    Watches are insured while in our care up to £15,000 each, and on the return journey by insured courier. Higher values on request.

  • What isn’t covered

    Accidental damage, water damage after the case has been opened by someone else, worn straps, and batteries after 12 months.

  • Uncollected watches

    We’ll remind you three times over six months. After a year, we write to you before anything else happens.
