The AI can retain important information about a user across conversations and bring that information back when useful.

Active Focus:
The AI agent is designed to continue pursuing a goal, change its approach when something fails, and adapt when it encounters new information.

RETENTION RULE
==================================================

Approximately every 5-8 seconds introduce something new:

CUT ON THE BEAT: every major cut in the first 15 seconds must land
on a beat or a music accent. If the music drops, the visual drops
with it. This is what makes it feel fun instead of corporate.

Add 2-3 REACTION-STYLE punch-ins per video: a fast 0.3s zoom into
the speaker's key word or a human face, like sports commentary.
Cheap, human, and it resets attention instantly.

Do not allow the viewer to become comfortable watching the same thing.

B-ROLL — FUN TO WATCH, NOT "AI LOOKING"
==================================================

ANTI-AI-LOOK RULE (strict):

If a shot could appear in ANY generic AI commercial, cut it.
The #1 thing that makes AI-news videos feel cheap is the same
recycled visuals: purple-blue neural gradients, glowing brains,
humanoid robots, floating hologram cities, matrix code rain.
BANNED, no exceptions.

Use those for nothing. Not even for half a second.

FUN B-ROLL RULE:

Every b-roll shot must feel REAL, HUMAN, or TACTILE.

Prefer (in order):
1. First-person POV: hands holding phone, over-the-shoulder laptop,
   screen recordings of an actual AI chat being used.
2. Human reaction moments: facepalm, raised eyebrow, leaning in,
   celebrating at a desk — real people, real rooms.
3. Tactile macro shots: coffee cup next to keyboard, fast typing,
   calendar pages, sticky notes, whiteboard scribbles.
4. Motion that matches the narration: a calendar flipping
   DAY 1 -> DAY 7 -> DAY 30 (speed-ramped), a progress bar
   physically crawling forward, papers flying off a desk.

For the memory section, stage it like a sports replay:
freeze the frame, draw a doodle arrow (broadcast style) pointing
at the memory that "reconnected."

Avoid generic office stock where everyone is smiling at a laptop.
Avoid slow-motion city flyovers. Avoid anything with a robot hand
touching a human hand.

Real > polished. A slightly shaky phone POV beats a perfect
CGI render every time for watch time.

Original list, still usable where it fits the rule above:
Meta headquarters / logo, developer coding, server rooms,
phone using AI assistant, close shots of keyboard / screen.
