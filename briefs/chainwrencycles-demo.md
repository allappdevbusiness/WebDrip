# Creative brief — Chainwren Cycles (chainwrencycles-demo)

Run date: 2026-10-04 · Business: neighbourhood bike repair workshop · Site style: TypeUI "riso" (warm paper, fluorescent pink, federal blue, offset print shadows, Space Grotesk + Overpass Mono)

## 0. Learning from the last run

Last run: Bobblefin Swim School (split-screen duel → POV phone browse). Its three biggest weaknesses, from its self-review and a cold re-read:

1. **Lines that needed explaining.** "Two swim schools. Ten seconds. One thumb.", "yeah, no" and "Lowkey just as gorgeous on a laptop" are cryptic or slangy. The voice rules now forbid them, so I removed the lesson that pushed punchy fragments.
2. **Mostly 2D motion.** Slides and springs everywhere. The card explode was the only real depth, which capped "cinematic" at 7.
3. **Small devices in the TikTok opening**, and a voiceover rushed to fit before the music's break.

This run also changes the format: there is **no voiceover**. A real song with a lead vocal plays on its own, and the story is told in big on-screen text.

Research focus this run: **editing to a vocal song** (letting the lyric and chorus land on story beats, cutting on phrases) plus **3D camera depth**. See research-notes.md.

## 1. Story angle: "The 9pm snapped chain"

Picture this: it's 9pm and a commuter's chain snaps on the way home. Your shop closed at six. They stand on the pavement and search for a bike repair shop on their phone. They find yours, but your website is one old page: no prices, "call for a quote", no way to book. Nobody answers at 9pm, so they book the shop across town. **Turn:** now picture your shop with a website that works after hours. They see every repair with its price, it is just as clear on the phone, and they book a tune-up and an 8am drop-off in a minute. **Payoff:** you open up the next morning to a booking that came in while you were closed. A good website keeps working after you close. WebDrip builds that.

One owner (the shop owner, addressed as "you"), one customer (the rider), one night. The "old site" is a generic, code-built mock with no name, logo or real business.

## 2. Story arc

1. **Problem (0–9 s):** a rider's chain snaps at 9pm. They find your shop online, but there are no prices and no way to book.
2. **Stakes (9–12 s):** they book the shop across town instead.
3. **Turn (12–16 s):** "Now picture this instead." This lands on the sung line "we can make it happen, turn things around".
4. **Proof (16–34 s):** the Chainwren site on the desktop, then on the phone, then the real booking form. Each line names one thing the viewer can see.
5. **Payoff (34–39 s):** the morning notification, then the lesson in one sentence. Then the WebDrip end card (39–45 s).

## 4. Edit style

**Clock-driven night-to-morning timeline.** A single time chip carries the story (9:04 PM → 9:06 PM → 7:58 AM), and the grade moves from a light dusk lavender to warm morning paper. Inside that frame there's a real **3D camera**: a crane rise on the opening phone, an orbit and dolly on the browser frame (rotateY within the tilt limits, always settling flat), and a card lift-out. It is not a split-screen duel, a feature tour or a POV browse. It's a mini-story with a clock.

## 5. Shot list (45 s at 30 fps; boundaries snap to the edit's beats after analysis)

1. **0.0–3.2 Hook.** Dusk-lavender canvas, faint dot grid. Big type, beat 1. Clock chip "9:04 PM" above it, digits ticking.
2. **3.2–9.4 The dead end.** A phone frame cranes up (rotateX 8°→0, settles flat) showing the code-built dated site. Beats 2–3. Push into the "Call for a quote" block, with a hand-drawn pink circle around "No prices".
3. **9.4–12.6 Booked elsewhere.** The phone desaturates and drops back, the clock ticks to 9:06 PM, and a small grey chip reads "Booked: a shop across town". Beat 4.
4. **12.6–17.0 The turn.** A rounded iris wipe expands from the clock chip, and the canvas warms to paper. Beat 5. The browser frame orbits in (rotateY 10°→0 feed / 6°→0 TikTok) with the real desktop hero recording (3D scroll camera).
5. **17.0–21.6 Every price.** The desktop clip scrolls into the prices grid. A slow push in, then two price tags pop out with a soft spotlight. Beat 6.
6. **21.6–25.8 On the phone.** Shared-element move: the browser slides back and a large phone (the real mobile scroll) rises in front. Beat 7.
7. **25.8–30.2 Booking.** The phone shows the real booking form being filled; the success check lands on the chorus downbeat. Beat 8.
8. **30.2–34.6 Morning.** The clock flips 9:07 PM → 7:58 AM and the canvas brightens. A notification card ("New booking · Full tune-up · Drop-off 8am") slides down above the phone. Beat 9.
9. **34.6–39.0 The lesson.** A bento collage (hero, spotlight, care plans tiles) animates in one by one. Beat 10.
10. **39.0–45.0 WebDrip end card.** Rounded mask wipe. Headline in three lines with a highlight stroke, the logo lands on the strongest late hit, then the button, then the follow line. Everything holds for 3 s or more while the music fades.

## 6. Look and sound

- **Camera language:** crane rise (rotateX), orbit (rotateY) and slow dolly push-ins (≤1.04) with perspective 1800 px. Every move eases out into a flat, square hold. Tilts are at most 10° on feed and 6° on TikTok, last under 0.6 s, then rest at 0.
- **Transition family:** *iris and shared-element*. A round iris wipe from the clock chip, a shared-element browser→phone handoff and a rounded mask wipe into the end card. No divider wipes or swipe-aways (that was last run).
- **Typography:** Space Grotesk 700 in sentence case, tight tracking (−0.035em), with the riso misregistration offset (pink shadow) on the key words of each line. Overpass Mono for the clock chip and small labels. Per-word mask slide-ups on the beat.
- **Color grade:** opens on a light dusk lavender (#E9E6F8) with cool blue text, then warms to riso paper (#FFF7EC) at the turn. Pink (#D61F7F) is the one accent for highlights and the progress line. Never dark.
- **Pacing curve:** calm and tense for the first 12 s (on the instrumental intro and soft verse), a lift at the pre-chorus turn, steady proof beats, the peak on the chorus (booking), then a warm settle on the end card.
- **Music:** soul with a male lead vocal, 95 BPM ("Links in a Chain", Chase Hughes feat. Michael Jaffe). The intro and verse sit under the problem, "we can make it happen, turn things around" under the turn, and the chorus "we are links in the chain" under the booking and the end card. New genre vs the last 3 runs (acoustic indie-pop, cinematic pop, warm cinematic).
- **Sound design (code-made, few, under the music):** one soft whoosh per camera move or transition, a light tick on each clock change, a soft pop for the price-tag lift-out and the notification, and a warm low impact when the WebDrip logo lands.

## 7. Better than last time

1. **Plain, complete sentences only** (fixes weakness 1). Every text beat passes a cold read (section 9), with no slang, fragments or jokes.
2. **Real 3D camera moves** (fixes weakness 2): a crane rise, an orbit plus dolly on the browser, and a lift-out, all settling flat before holds.
3. **Big devices and big type on TikTok from frame 1** (fixes weakness 3): the phone fills about 75% of the usable height and the text lane sits above it. The song's own lyric ("turn things around") lands on the story's turn (research takeaway).

It's more cinematic because of the depth moves and the night-to-morning grade. It's more gripping because the clock gives the viewer a reason to keep watching. It sells better because the payoff ("a booking that came in while you were closed") is a concrete, everyday win.

**Cold read (as a stranger who has never heard of WebDrip or this shop):** every line is one complete, plain thought a person would say out loud. There is no slang, no pun and no fragment that needs explaining. The only short fragment, "It's 9pm.", sets the scene and is followed straight away by the event. Beat 4 originally ended with "instead", which repeated beat 5's word, so it now reads "So they book the shop across town." The text hook ("If you fix bikes for a living, read this.") differs from the caption hook ("What happens when a customer needs you after closing time?"). **Passed.**

**Music brief (logged):** warm, hopeful soul with a male lead vocal, about 95 BPM: a soft intro and verse under the after-hours problem, the pre-chorus line "turn things around" on the story's turn, and the chorus under the booking payoff and the WebDrip logo.

Nothing scored under 7, so the post gate passes (story clarity 9 and text readability 8, both ≥ 7). Fixed before the final render: the phone crane starting off-canvas, the dolly not settled at the first hold, a 3-line TikTok text beat, the TikTok clock card touching the button column, and three near-static holds (added a slow dolly drift, a page auto-scroll and a floating notification).

**What worked:** a clock chip as the story device; using the song's own lyric for the turn; a code-built dated site; the shared iris from the chip into the "after" world; a real booking recording with the success check landing on a beat.

**Top 3 for the next run:**
1. One long, slow orbit (2–3 s) around a device that settles flat, instead of only short tilts, for more true depth.
2. Keep every hold under 2.5 s, or give it a visible change (a new element, a scroll), especially in the "stakes" act.
3. Make the "before" moment more visual on TikTok (a bigger push into the problem, or a side-by-side with the after) so it reads even at a glance.
